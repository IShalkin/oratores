# Sources

The corpus this package was synthesized from: 38 works, spanning the classical rhetorical canon, modern speechwriting and presentation practice, persuasion science, diffusion and change research, and propaganda analysis.

**What this list is.** A record of what fed the synthesis, so that a claim can be traced to a tradition and so that a maintainer can tell when a module is missing a contribution. Each entry names the edition or translation actually used, because both change the content materially, and records the known defect where the source had one.

**What this list is not.** A bibliography attached to the modules. The modules do not cite these works, deliberately — the package **is** the synthesis, and a claim that needs an exact citation with chapter and page requires the source artifact at the time of the claim. That is `SRC-01`, and without the artifact the claim is `UNVERIFIED` or is restated as synthesis. See [source-provenance.md](../skills/oratores/references/source-provenance.md).

Where per-book source packs are installed alongside this skill, they are evidence lookups under `SRC-01` and never task modules.

## Propaganda, crowds and mass persuasion

Included because these are the works that document the mechanisms most precisely — what each one does, on whom, how fast, and what it costs over time — and because recognition is at least as useful as deployment. Several of them advocate positions the package does not take; they are here for their analysis of the machinery, which remains the best available.

| Work | Principal contribution |
|---|---|
| Jowett & O'Donnell, *Propaganda and Persuasion* (6th ed., 2015) | judge by purpose, not by truth-value — accurate material can still be propaganda; white/gray/black; the ten-step analysis behind `MEC-04`; the laundering source models; resonance and anchoring |
| Ellul, *Propaganda: The Formation of Men's Attitudes* | the four classification axes; orthopraxy rather than orthodoxy; pre-propaganda; truth of fact with falsehood of intention; fundamental currents; the five conditions; why education is a prerequisite rather than an antidote; means deforming ends |

## Source-edition notes worth carrying forward

- **Cialdini, *Influence*** — the pack used is the six-principle revised edition. It does **not** contain Unity; that principle arrives in *Pre-Suasion*, which is where `PTH-05` and `MEC-01` get it from.
- **Cialdini, *Pre-Suasion*** — the pack used is the **Spanish-language edition**. English terminology in the modules is reconstructed and is not quotable. Any exact citation needs the English text.
- **Jowett & O'Donnell** — 6th ed. (2015), American spelling. The definition is quoted exactly in `MEC-01`'s structure because the word-by-word parse is the tool.
- **Rogers** — 5th ed. Rogers himself is sceptical that the adopter categories are discontinuous; Moore's chasm asserts a discontinuity between two of them. `CHG-05` carries both and does not pretend they agree.
- **Aristotle, Cicero, Quintilian, Longinus** — all from public-domain nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century translations of optical scans. Wording differs materially from modern translations. `SRC-01` names this hazard specifically: do not blend two translations into one quotation, and name the translation when the wording is the point.
- **Churchill, *Scaffolding*** and **Cicero, *Orator*** — the two thinnest sources in the corpus. Their contributions are real but narrow, and they carry less weight in the synthesis than their prominence would suggest.
