# Invention — INV

Producing the raw material: the lines, images, openings, arguments, examples, jokes, titles and stories that every other module in this package then selects, orders, tests or cuts. Nothing here judges. This is the only module whose output is allowed to be bad.

The governing fact: **a method can only tell you where to dig.** A catalogue of topics, a list of devices, a set of prompts — each marks ground where arguments and images lie buried, and none of them hands you one. Genius is given and method is small; what converts a mark on the ground into material is care and repeated attempts, and that is the one variable of the three you control.

The corollary that shapes the module: **the strong candidate is almost never the first one.** It surfaces after four or five have percolated up, and the first one is the cliché. An author who judges while generating therefore keeps the worst thing they thought of, because it was the only thing they let themselves think.

**A note on the form.** Where a source states a failure mode or a cost for a generator, it is here. Where the literature states none, the line is absent, and an absent line means unknown rather than free.

---

## INV-01 — Fix what is actually in dispute

**Trigger.** Before any material is generated. Again whenever a generating session produces nothing usable.

**Procedure.**

1. Every persuasive act terminates in a decision by someone who has not yet made it. Name that person and name the decision. A single listener, a spectator at a ceremony and a board are all deciding, and the demands do not relax for the informal case. Where there is no live opponent, personify the counter-case and answer it as though it had a mouth.
2. **There is no arguing what is already settled.** List what this audience already accepts and treat it as premise, not as content. Material generated against a conceded point is effort spent where nobody is standing, and it reads as padding.
3. Run the three questions in order and stop at the first one your audience actually contests. That rung is where all your material has to come from:
   - **Did it happen?** — a dispute about fact. Generate records, witnesses, opportunity, motive.
   - **What is it?** — the fact is conceded and the name is contested. Generate definitions, boundary cases, what the term does and does not cover.
   - **What kind of thing is it?** — fact and name are conceded and the quality is contested. Generate justification, circumstance, provocation, precedent, equity.
   - There is a fourth move, sideways: **should this be decided here at all?** Nothing about the facts is in dispute and the venue, the standing, the procedure or the timing is.
   Marshalling proof that something happened when the audience has conceded it and is arguing about what it means is the classic waste, and no quantity of good material repairs a piece aimed at the wrong rung.
4. Name the axis the decision is judged on, because it decides which of your material is even admissible: **just or unjust** where the question is what was done; **expedient — and honourable, which is the stronger half** where the question is what to do next; **honourable or base** where the question is what something is worth. Arguing on the wrong axis is the commonest strategic error available.
5. Add the four questions every case needs regardless of rung. Each is a separate generating prompt and each yields different material: is it **possible**; did it in fact **happen**; will it in fact **happen**; and **how large** is it.
6. **Narrow the subject before you generate anything.** Material arrives far more readily against a specific phase of a subject than against the subject. "Law" yields nothing; "a poor man cannot afford to prosecute" yields a piece. A stalled session is very often a scope problem wearing the costume of a talent problem.
7. Write the rung, the axis and the narrowed subject in one place before opening any generator. This is the difference between a generating session and a pleasant hour.

**Gates.**
- The decider and the undecided part of the decision are written down.
- What the audience already concedes is listed, and nothing is generated against it.
- The contested rung is written down, together with the rungs conceded.
- The value axis is named in writing.
- The subject is narrowed to a stated phase, not left at the category.

**Failure signals.**
- Material piled on a point nobody disputes.
- A case about what something means, built as a case about whether it happened.
- "The topic is X" written where the contested question belongs.
- A generating session opened on a subject broad enough to be a book title.

## INV-02 — Stock the material before the occasion

**Trigger.** Continuously, as a standing habit — and deliberately the moment a piece has to be produced and the shelf is bare.

**Procedure.**

1. Split preparation in two and keep both running. **General** preparation is what you have put in over years: read, seen, argued about, been changed by. **Special** preparation is the gathering and shaping for one occasion. A piece prepared only specially is thin in a way editing cannot reach, because what looks like invention on the day is retrieval from what you stored, and there is nothing there to retrieve.
2. Keep a **written stock file indexed by subject rather than by source.** One entry per subject, and against it where the material actually sits — the clipping, the volume and the page, your own earlier draft. Every good line, statistic, litany, joke and quotation you meet or invent goes in on the day you meet it. Unfiled material is gone inside a week, and the loss is invisible: you never learn what you failed to remember.
3. Mine the wells nobody else has. Personal material implies experience where borrowed material implies only reading, and it is the one category a rival cannot copy:
   - **people you have actually met or heard** — and what they actually said
   - **where you are from** — who else was born there, or passed through
   - **places you have physically stood** — a monument, a factory floor, a ward, a battlefield
   - **what you did on your own time** — travel, sport, a film, a repair
   Recall the encounter or the visit that impressed you most, then say precisely what impressed you, and interrogate it until it is tellable in ninety seconds. Every item still passes `NAR-02` before it enters a draft.
4. Recover stories by breaking chronology instead of searching it. Walk the **people** — sketch who was connected to whom by what situation, including the people outside any hierarchy. Walk the **places** — go room by room through the spaces you have lived and worked in, drawing the details until the forgotten ones return. Walk the **things** — catalogue the objects that mean something and what happened around each. Tag each recovered story with the feeling it carries, so a future piece can find it by the emotion it needs.
5. Train the observation the whole stock depends on. Convert the day's incidents into possible illustrations as they happen, and test yourself by describing what you saw in definite words. If you cannot, you did not see it. The standard is the small specific detail nobody else would have kept.
6. Read with the acquisitive question — **what can I take from this?** — not "is this any good?" File each model under one nameable, transferable excellence, so it can be retrieved when a piece needs that particular thing.
7. **Read the side you disagree with, deliberately.** A stock built only from congenial material gives you excellent models and a permanent blind spot: you learn to handle every challenge adversarially, because you have never watched the other case made well by somebody who believed it.
8. Budget the time where the scarcity is. Facts, positions and analysis will be supplied by other people. The story, the analogy, the concrete detail and the joke will not — nobody else produces them, and they are what the audience keeps. A day spent finding one right detail against an hour spent writing the draft is the correct ratio, not a symptom of avoidance.
9. Keep the discards. What you cut from one piece is stock for the next, and the file is also what makes cutting bearable — `REV-01`.

**Gates.**
- A written stock file exists, indexed by subject.
- This job added at least one entry to it.
- Each model in the reading stock is filed under one named transferable excellence.
- At least one item in the stock came from a source that disagrees with the speaker.
- The carrying material — story, analogy, detail, joke, quotation — has hours in the plan, stated separately from the drafting hours.

**Failure signals.**
- Preparation that begins when the invitation is accepted.
- A search for a story starting the evening before.
- A quotation dictionary opened under deadline. Nothing looks usable under the gun.
- Every model in the stock agreeing with you.
- "I'll remember that."

## INV-03 — Diverge: run the generators without judging

**Trigger.** Any piece needing a line, an image, an opening, an argument, an example, a joke, a title or a story — so, any piece.

**Procedure.**

1. **Leave the drafting tool.** A presentation application, a slide template and a document outline are containers for material, not producers of it. Open one to think and your thinking narrows to whatever that tool makes easy, and you end up executing the idea that was cheapest to build rather than the one that communicates. Generate on paper or on a wall, and change the room if you can.
2. **One candidate per card, written with a marker.** The size is the test, not the format: material that will not fit on a card, or needs finer detail than a marker gives, is too complex and has to be split or rethought before it goes anywhere near a draft.
3. **Chase quantity, and refuse to judge while you are chasing it.** Push through three or four full rounds. Keep the pace fast enough that nobody — you included — has time to self-censor. Write down the candidates you already know are bad, because a bad one is frequently what the next one is built out of.
4. Record the **first** candidate in each set and mark it as first. Assume it is the cliché. It usually is, and it is the one an unsupervised author keeps.
5. Then run the sets, chosen by what you need:

| What you need | Generator |
|---|---|
| A coined line | **antonym pairs** — write out the opposites actually in play (buyer/seller, leader/follower, lender/borrower, ends/means, givers/takers, winner/loser) and combine them |
| | **rhyme families** — take the operative word, run the one-syllable family (earn/learn/yearn/turn; gain/pain/wane/retain; sell/tell/fell/well), and keep a rhyming dictionary within reach. If the word rhymes badly, substitute a near-synonym and run the alphabet against that instead |
| | **repeated word** — cross the twelve commonest monosyllabic verbs (bear, bring, come, get, give, make, put, reach, sit, stand, take, work) with the commonest adverbs (across, down, for, in, out, over, to, under, up, with, without) and read the collisions aloud |
| | **consonant clusters** — list the words in your subject sharing an opening consonant, with a thesaurus to extend each list; consonants bite where vowels do not |
| A central metaphor | **word-map** — write the theme, radiate word associations from it, then keep going past the point where you already have an answer |
| | **one domain, walked** — pick a source domain and go through it item by item rather than waiting for the image to arrive. The domain does the work; `LNG-03` holds which domain to pick and what it imports |
| An opening | the reference inventory in `OPN-01`; the honest-openings list in `OPN-02` where the room is tired of the subject |
| An argument | **the topic scan** — a fixed list of questions run over the case in order, as a search rather than as inspiration. Set out below |
| An example | **found parallel** — a real precedent, projected forward. Harder to find and worth more, because it happened |
| | **built parallel** — an invented case with the same structure, moved into a domain nobody has a stake in: appointing athletes by lot, choosing the pilot by lot. Easy to devise, and it must end in an item-by-item mapping back onto the real case or it proves nothing. Keep the borrowed domain uncontested, or you have doubled your burden of proof rather than displaced it |
| A picture | **the six senses** — run the thing through sight, sound, movement, taste, smell and touch in turn, then combine two or three. Most of an audience thinks in pictures and some do not, so a mixed passage reaches each hearer more than once |
| | **statement into scene** — take the general claim and rewrite it as one specific happening, two people, a named room, a particular day. Then push past what you actually witnessed into what plausibly followed, and mark where the witnessed part stops |
| A joke | **clustering** — list everything associated with one person, place or group; list the same for a second; collide the lists. The laugh is in the item that is out of place |
| | **"if this is true, what else is true?"** — take the premise and carry it to its most absurd end |
| | **the disconnect** — write what the group represents, how it sees itself, and how everyone else sees it. The humour lives in the gaps between the three |
| | **defeated expectation** — establish a pattern in two beats and break it in the third. Most of the other joke shapes run on this one |
| | **borrowing** — you are not required to invent jokes. A line of unknown origin needs no source; a living source is named, which also signals to the room that a joke is coming |
| A title | mine the finished body for a phrase; then allusion — aphorism, mythology, scripture, literature, sport the audience actually follows; then the devices: alliteration, a triad, an inversion of a phrase everyone knows. Whatever you pick, the piece ties it to the subject in its first minutes, not on its last page |
| Contrast material | for every claim write its **intelligent** counter-argument, then run the exercise again in reverse order, which triggers more. Populate both sides or the contrast collapses — `MSG-04` |
| A story | the people, places and things walk in `INV-02`; then `NAR-02` |

6. **The topic scan**, since it is the set that most reliably produces argument nobody in the room had. Run the questions; do not read them. What is the **contrary**, and does the claim hold of it? What follows if the word is inflected — just, justly, justice? If the doer acted rightly, did the sufferer suffer rightly? Does it hold **more** where it would be more natural, or fail **less** where it would be less? What did they say or do **earlier**, and does that square with now? Can their own words be turned back on this case? What does the disputed term actually **mean**, and in which of its senses are you arguing? What are all the **alternatives**, and can you eliminate them? What **parallel instances** exist? What has been **decided before**, on this point or a like one, especially by people it would be indecent to contradict? Break the whole into **parts** and challenge each. What **follows** from the thing, good and bad — and from its opposite, good and bad? What do people say about this in public and think in private? Who **benefits**? What **better course** was open and not taken? What **inconsistency** is there in the dates, the acts, the statements? What innocent **cause** would account for the damaging appearance? Is it **implausible and therefore probably true**, since nobody would have imagined it otherwise?
   Two of these are known-defective and carry a caveat before an alert audience: the sufferer's deserving does not follow from the doer's being right, and a better course is far easier to see after the event than before it.
7. Run the sets **solo, then as a group, then solo again.** The closing solo pass is where the ideas the group half-said actually develop. For the group pass: about five people, mixed in background and skill; one candidate per card; every candidate treated as worth having; and watch the senior person whose presence quietly narrows what anyone offers. One dismissive remark from a superior silences that participant for the rest of the session, and catching and re-opening the squashed contribution is the moderator's whole job.

**The literature holds two positions on how a candidate should arrive, and the condition that selects between them is known: the two are about different objects.**

**Free association**, where you are generating **expressive material** — a line, an image, an opening, a joke, a title. There is nothing to enumerate; the strong candidate arrives because weak ones preceded it, and quantity is the only route past the obvious. Judgement withheld until the well is dry.

**Structural enumeration**, where you are generating a **hypothesis that has to be true or false** — a cause, a diagnosis, an explanation of a result nobody can account for. Here free association is named as the failure, because it produces candidates nothing can test. Enumerate the structural elements of the situation instead, imagine a modification of each, and sharpen the list by looking for something in the world that behaves the same way. The test of a candidate so generated is whether an observation could exclude it; one that no observation can kill is not a hypothesis. Then run `CHG-01` and `MSG-03`.

Mixing the two is how you get the confident diagnosis nobody can check, and the earnest line nobody remembers.

**Gates.**
- Generation happened away from the drafting tool, on paper or a wall.
- Every set has at least five recorded candidates before any is judged.
- The first candidate in each set is recorded and marked as first.
- Nothing was refused during the generating pass.
- Each set names the generator that produced it.
- Any hypothesis-shaped candidate carries the observation that would exclude it.

**Failure signals.**
- Three candidates and a decision.
- The generating session held inside the deck.
- A candidate list with the bad ones already deleted — nobody can now tell whether the survivor won or was the only entrant.
- A built parallel with no mapping back onto the real case.
- A group session in which the most senior person spoke first.
- A cause named by association and never tested.

## INV-04 — Converge: choose the survivor

**Trigger.** The generating pass is finished and the well is dry. Not before.

**Procedure.**

1. **Switch passes deliberately, and say that you are switching.** Generation and selection are different acts in different postures, and running them together produces the worst of both: you censor what you have not finished thinking and keep what you have not finished testing. Cycle as often as you like — diverge, converge, diverge again — and never blend.
2. Filter everything against the one governing idea (`MSG-01`) before applying any test of quality. A brilliant candidate that does not serve the thesis is the hardest thing in this discipline to throw away and the thing most reliably worth throwing away. If you do not filter, the audience must, and they will resent it.
3. **Weigh, do not count.** Discard the trifling and the duplicative outright. A candidate that helps a little while dragging a liability is a net loss — `LOG-01`.
4. Then apply the test belonging to the kind:

| Kind | Test |
|---|---|
| Any line meant to be remembered | **the paraphrase test** — reword it. If it survives, the effect sits in the thought: it can be translated, shortened, re-delivered and handed to a speaker to say their own way. If it dies, the effect sits in the wording — mark it, and let nobody touch it in revision |
| Any line at all | **the quick-learning band** — it must hand the hearer something they did not have, at a distance they can cross almost at once. Too remote and they cannot see it at a glance; too familiar and there is nothing to get. Both ends are dead, and the familiar end is the more common |
| A flat line you cannot diagnose | **the three dials** — a line that lands runs on contrast, on transferred meaning, or on the thing shown as happening now. A flat one usually has all three at zero |
| Anything surprising | **post-dictability** — unpredictable before, obvious after. Surprise that yields no insight is a gimmick and costs more than it buys; `MSG-02` |
| An image | **the exit test** — an image loses its force the moment it grants the audience a way out of the thing being described. One plank between them and the water is weaker than the danger stated with no limit at all |
| A metaphor | the entailment and extension tests in `LNG-03` |
| A joke | roughly one listener in three finding it funny is enough, because the people beside them laugh too. Then the two constraints: aim it at yourself or your own side first, and singe rather than burn |
| A story or example | the five criteria in `NAR-02` |
| A reference or a quotation | the familiarity test for *this* audience — `OPN-01`, `LNG-05` |
| Any candidate at all | do you actually think it is good? Every line and every slide has to be necessary **and** genuinely interesting. Anything in the maybe pile is out |

5. Respect the counts the rest of the package sets, because the counts are part of what makes a kept candidate land: one coined line, one central metaphor, one quotation, one designed moment. Two of anything cancel — the audience keeps one and does not consult you about which.
6. **Record the refusals as refusals.** Against every candidate write kept, refused or still open, and keep the refused ones visible rather than deleted. A candidate killed by a note in prose is a candidate somebody downstream will use, including you next week. Record separately whether the first candidate was thrown away; if it never is, you are not generating, you are justifying.
7. Accept the destruction. You produced many candidates precisely so all but one could go without regret, and losing the rest is the process working rather than waste. Let the rough sketch be the cost filter it is: material that is painful to rough out is worse to build.

**The literature holds two positions on the first candidate, and the condition that selects between them is who has heard it.**

**Refuse it** where it is the answer anyone would have given — the handshake over the globe, the journey with no destination, a definition of the thing the room is already tired of hearing about. It arrived first because it is common property, which is exactly why it lands on nobody.

**Reuse it** where the material is yours and this audience has not heard it. Good lines do not grow on trees, and your own exhaustion with a line is not the audience's: most of the room is hearing it for the first time, and the part that is not came partly to hear it again. Proven material is also the only kind whose timing is already rehearsed, which is most of why it works — a line that needs months to time cannot be timed in ten days.

The two collide only when the tested material is also the obvious material. Then it is a cliché with a good service record, and it goes.

**Gates.**
- The convergent pass is a separate, named pass.
- Every candidate carries kept, refused or open — refusals recorded as refusals, not as prose notes.
- Whether the first candidate was refused is recorded.
- Every kept candidate has the test named for its kind written against it.
- Anything that fails the paraphrase test is marked so that it is never reworded.
- The counts the downstream modules set are respected.

**Failure signals.**
- One candidate presented as a decision.
- The refused candidates deleted, so the record proves nothing.
- A kept candidate whose test you cannot name.
- Two memorable lines, two central metaphors, or three quotations.
- "It's the only one I could think of" offered as a reason to keep it.
- A line whose effect was in the wording, rewritten in editing, and now flat.

## INV-05 — When nothing good arrives

**Trigger.** Two or three generating rounds have produced nothing you would keep, or the deadline is close and the page is blank.

**Procedure.**

1. Name which of the three shortages you have, in writing, because they take different remedies. **No material** — the stock is empty. **No question** — you do not know what is in dispute. **No permission** — you are judging while generating. Only the first is about invention at all: for the second run `INV-01`, for the third `INV-03`.
2. **Narrow the subject further.** The cheapest move available and the most reliably effective. The general theme withholds material that a specific phase of it releases immediately.
3. **Go and get something first-hand.** Talk to a person on the ground rather than reading about them; go back to the actual place, record or photograph rather than trusting your memory of it. The usable line reliably comes from a specific human source outside the briefing material, and reliably does not come from reading more of the briefing material.
4. **Interview the speaker adversarially**, with a written list of questions, and argue with the answers. *I don't understand that. Can you say it in simpler terms? Can you give me an example? Does that have any practical application? Why does this matter to the people who will be in the room? Are you sure that is the right position?* Then wait for "you'd never use this in a speech, but —" and start writing. That sentence is where the material is. `ETH-04`.
5. **Write anyway, by hand.** Writing produces material rather than recording it: putting an idea into words generates the next idea, and the apparent invention of a fluent speaker is retrieval from what they previously wrote down. On the day, write hot and revise cold — `REV-01`. Over a career, the order reverses: write carefully and slowly, and let speed arrive as the residue of care, because starting from speed produces fluency with nothing under it.
6. **Argue the other side in full.** Compose the opposing case as though you held it, at its best strength, then reverse the order of its points and do it again. This produces your own counter-arguments and the contrast material `MSG-04` needs, and it is the standard remedy for a case you cannot see past.
7. **Imitate deliberately, and from several models rather than one.** Ask how a speaker you admire would have handled this passage — and, harder and more useful, how they would have listened to what you have already written. Take method, judgement and arrangement, never diction: what they did with the case, not their sentences. Single-model imitation transmits the model's faults along with its virtues and leaves you one register for every occasion. A copy is inferior to its original by construction, so imitation is a stage; the destination is supplying what your models lack and cutting what they overdo.
8. **Raid your own discards and your own past pieces.** The file of what you cut is the first place to look and last year's piece is the second.
9. Where the remaining hole is a **fact** — a figure, a study, a case, an attribution — stop generating. That gap is the finding. Write it as a gap naming what belongs there and who holds it, deliver everything not depending on it, and never fill it with something plausible. An invented figure is false work product and separately the fastest way to lose a room, and an empty page against a deadline is precisely the condition under which it gets invented. `MEC-03`, `SRC-01`.
10. Where nothing arrives because there is nothing to say, the honest outputs are a shorter piece, a different form, or a declined slot — `OCC-01`. Do not fill the time with the noise of having no ideas. And accept the standing terms of the work: nothing worth having is produced quickly, difficulty comes before the result, and a piece with real reach and a visible flaw beats a safe piece with nothing in it.

**Gates.**
- Which of the three shortages is in play is named in writing.
- The subject has been narrowed at least once.
- At least one first-hand source — a person, a place, a record — was consulted before the third attempt at the line.
- The discard file and the previous pieces have been searched.
- The opposing case has been written out at full strength.
- Every remaining hole is either filled from a real source or written as a gap naming what belongs there and who holds it.

**Failure signals.**
- More reading of the same briefing material.
- A quotation dictionary opened an hour before the deadline.
- A figure that appeared without a source while the page was blank.
- A plausible anecdote whose participants you cannot name.
- Waiting for the idea instead of writing.
- Hedges, wind-ups and throat-clearing where material should be — noise standing in for having something to say.

## Defaults and thresholds

| Decision | Default |
|---|---|
| Candidates per set before any judging | at least 5 |
| Rounds of divergence | 3–4 |
| Where generation happens | paper or a wall, never the drafting tool |
| Candidates per note | exactly 1; if it will not fit, the idea is too complex |
| Judgement during the generating pass | none |
| First candidate | recorded, marked as first, assumed to be the cliché |
| Group session size | about 5, mixed backgrounds |
| Session sequence | solo → group → solo |
| Joke usability threshold | roughly one listener in three |
| Refusals | recorded as refusals, never as prose notes |
| Finding material against drafting | a day for one right detail against an hour drafting is normal, not avoidance |
| Point candidates to generate, then pare | `MSG-05` |
| Invented facts | never; the gap is the finding |

## Related

`AUD-01` fixes the change this material has to produce; `MSG-01` is the filter every candidate is measured against and `MSG-05` decides how many survive. `LNG-02` and `LNG-03` select and place the lines and images produced here. `OPN-01` and `OPN-03` take the openings and closes; `NAR-02` gates the stories; `NAR-04` places the one designed moment. `LOG-01` and `LOG-03` take the arguments, order them, and decide how many are needed. `MSG-04` needs both sides of the contrast generated here. `REV-01` cuts what survives and supplies the discard file `INV-05` raids. `MEC-03` and `SRC-01` govern the case where the missing material is a fact.
