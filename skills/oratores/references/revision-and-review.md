# Revision and Review — REV

Editing, cutting, and the audit before delivery. The premise: a first draft is a discovery exercise, and the piece that gets delivered is made in the fourth or fifth pass. Expect to lose most of what you wrote — a long draft reduces to about a third of itself before it is any good, and life usually arrives on a later pass than anyone budgets for.

---

## REV-01 — The editing passes

**Trigger.** A complete draft exists. Let it rest first if you can — a few days, or overnight at minimum.

**Procedure.** Four passes, in order. Do not merge them; merging is why editing produces polish rather than improvement.

**Pass 1 — Cut.**
- Repetition that is not deliberate.
- Hedges: *somewhat, arguably, relatively, to some extent, it could be argued.*
- Wind-ups: *what I want to talk about today is, it is worth noting that, I think it is fair to say.*
- Flourish that serves no function.
- Passages imposed by someone whose approval you needed and that break the thesis.
- Jokes that are not funny to this audience.
- Anything borrowed that you have not credited. You will be caught.
- Anything you cannot state the purpose of.
Set a numeric target — trim a tenth, then another — and go sentence by sentence. Cut whatever matters more to you than to the audience. Keep a file of what you removed; it makes the cutting bearable and the material is reusable.

**Pass 2 — Replace.**
- Jargon, acronyms, and unpronounceable names.
- Weasel words and abstractions carrying a load.
- Concept nouns, replaced with what actually happens.
- Passive constructions where the actor should be named. See `LNG-01`.
- Clichés, and any metaphor you have heard this quarter.

**Pass 3 — Improve.**
- Connotation: for two words with the same meaning, take the one that sounds like what you mean.
- Sound: soft consonants for reassurance, hard ones for decision.
- Position: the strongest phrase at the end of the sentence, the strongest sentence at the end of the section.
- Rhythm: vary sentence length deliberately across the piece.
- Place the figures. See `LNG-02`. Then remove one — there is usually one too many.

**Pass 4 — Check.**
- Does the argument still hold after the cuts? Cutting reliably removes a link.
- Does the register still fit the occasion?
- Length, read aloud and timed.
- Every number, quotation and attribution verified against its source.
- Clearance: anything that needs legal, regulatory or client sign-off.

**Gates.**
- Four passes run separately.
- A cut target set and met.
- Argument re-checked after cutting.
- Every number and attribution verified.

**Failure signals.**
- One pass that does everything, producing a smoother draft of the same piece.
- Editing on screen only, never aloud.
- A draft that grew during editing.
- "I'll check that figure later."

## REV-02 — Structural audit

**Trigger.** After editing, before rehearsal.

**Procedure.**

1. Re-run the pyramid rules from `MSG-03` on the edited version. Editing breaks groupings; a piece that passed before drafting frequently fails after.
2. **Horizontal logic**: read only the section headings or slide titles, in order. Do they tell the story on their own? If they read as a table of contents, the titles are topics and not conclusions.
3. **Vertical logic**: is each section internally self-reinforcing, with nothing in it that belongs elsewhere?
4. **Reverse-storyboard**: flip through the finished artifact and write down each page's or section's main point. Compare the list to your intended outline. Where they diverge, the artifact is not what you think it is — and the list is right.
5. Re-check the arc: compare the first minute to the last. Has the audience moved?
6. Re-check the beats: has editing removed the emotional passages and left the analytical ones? This is the standard casualty of a cutting pass, because the analytical material looks more defensible when you are deciding what to lose.
7. Re-check the ask. Is it still specific, still owned, still dated? Asks get softened in review more often than any other element.
8. Count the memorable moments. There should be exactly one.

**Gates.**
- Pyramid rules re-run post-edit.
- Horizontal and vertical logic pass.
- Reverse storyboard matches the intent.
- The ask survived review intact.

**Failure signals.**
- Titles that describe rather than assert.
- The emotional content gone and nobody noticed.
- Two memorable moments, or none.
- An ask that now reads "we should consider".

## REV-03 — Pre-delivery audit

**Trigger.** Before declaring any piece finished. This is the gate.

**Procedure.** Run all six sections. A failure in any one is a finding, and a Critical finding forbids a ready verdict.

**Strategic clarity**
- Is the objective clear, and is the desired behaviour named?
- Is the thesis sayable in one sentence, without a visual?
- Does anything in it matter to this audience specifically?
- Is there any generic language left where something specific belongs?

**Audience fit**
- Is the audience's actual position acknowledged, including fatigue and scepticism?
- Are the segments handled, or is this one message to a split room?
- Is each addressed person's own interest visible to them?
- Does anything in it require knowledge they do not have?

**Persuasive completeness**
- Is credibility established in the piece, not assumed from the speaker's title?
- Is there emotional content, and is it proportionate?
- Is there evidence, and is it the kind this audience accepts?
- Are the objections handled, with the limitations conceded?
- Is any social proof real?

**Narrative**
- Is there real tension, and is it named?
- Is there one human or client-level story?
- Is the contrast between now and later actually populated on both sides?
- Is there a turning point?
- Is there one memorable line?
- Does the close land?

**Actionability**
- Is there a specific next step?
- Is there an owner?
- Is there a date?
- Is there a metric?
- Is there a mechanism for it to travel beyond the room?
- Is there a follow-up?

**Exposure** — run `MEC-03`, and specifically:
- Is any number invented, unsourced, or a best case presented as an expectation?
- Is any forecast phrased as a fact, any pilot as a rollout, any demo as a proof?
- Is every number, quotation and attribution traceable to something you could produce within a day?
- Are the limits stated somewhere, or only in the notes?
- Which claim in here would be the first one a hostile reader checked, and does it hold?
- Which mechanism is doing the work, and what happens to the piece if it is named out loud?

Then, if the piece asks a leadership audience for adoption, run the five-question test from `EXE-04`. A piece can satisfy every checklist above and still fail it.

**Output format.** Findings first, ranked worst-first, each anchored to a specific line, section or slide, each with: what is wrong in one sentence, the concrete failure it produces, and the smallest fix. Then what you checked and found clean, so the scope of the review is legible — an unstated scope reads as total coverage. Then a verdict, only if one was asked for.

**Gates.**
- All six sections run.
- Every finding anchored to a location.
- Clean areas listed explicitly.
- The five-question test run where it applies.

**Failure signals.**
- "Looks good" or "feels weak" as a review.
- A finding with no location.
- A verdict issued before the findings.
- A review that rewrote the piece instead of reporting on it.

## REV-04 — After delivery

**Trigger.** The piece has been delivered. Most people skip this, which is why most people's next piece is no better.

**Procedure.**

1. Judge it by what the audience said in the corridor afterwards, not by how the speaker felt on the way home. The two are unrelated with startling reliability.
2. Capture, on the day: what was committed and by whom; which line got quoted back; which question you could not answer; where the room's attention visibly went; what you cut on the fly and whether you missed it.
3. Watch the recording, if there is one, once.
4. Feed the unanswerable question into the objection map, and the quoted line into the reusable module stock. See `CMP-01`.
5. Where a claim turned out to be wrong, correct it explicitly and once, and stop repeating it. See `MEC-03`.
6. Where the piece will be delivered again, revise before the memory of the room fades — not the week before the next occasion.
7. Follow up on the commitments. Nothing else determines whether the piece did anything. See `ACT-02`.

**Gates.**
- Commitments captured on the day.
- One review pass while the memory is fresh.
- Objection map and module stock updated.
- Follow-up owned by someone.

**Failure signals.**
- No record of what was committed.
- The same unanswerable question twice.
- A piece delivered five times without revision.
- A correction that never happened.

## Defaults and thresholds

| Decision | Default |
|---|---|
| Editing passes | 4, separate |
| Draft shrinkage | expect to lose roughly two-thirds |
| Pass where it becomes good | 4 or 5 |
| Rest before editing | overnight minimum, a few days if available |
| Cut target | 10% at a time, repeatedly |
| Read aloud | every pass |
| Memorable moments after edit | exactly 1 |
| Findings format | worst-first, anchored, with the smallest fix |
| Post-delivery review | same day |
